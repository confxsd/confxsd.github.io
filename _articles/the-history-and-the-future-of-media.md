When the first humans opened their eyes, they invented a place. Looking like the world, feeling like the world. We called it… the World?

Really?

Now we know it’s just modeling “the” world, literally, optimizing some parameters, balancing the weights. Helmholtz already described this as “unconscious inference” in 1867. The brain never touches the world. I know, it’s almost an inevitable metaphysical conclusion. What a disappointment for our generation.

Sound was the first medium, ephemeral yet magical. I listen to a lot of music, and almost every time, surprised like how could we achieve such a surreal production capability.

Text came next. It was super efficient, persistent, and helped unlock the entire scientific journey. We built history, literature, laws, institutions… It also ignited the most narcissistic period of human history. I mean the “humanist” period, humanity discovered itself by consuming ancestors outputs, and felt like they ruled the world. But that’s not the point.

Probably the most exciting development was the infrastructure of fantasies and desires, almost an imagination, a creativity machine. Visual arts evolved fast, blended with accelerating tech progress, and took the form we consume every day: moving pictures, video, film.

Like, no creature experiences “the” world directly. Each species lives inside the slice of reality its senses can register. Each new medium gives humans a new perceptual world, continuous with how seeing already works.

Jakob von Uexküll described this as the Umwelt, the organism’s own perceptual world, in 1909. André Leroi-Gourhan argued in 1964 that human evolution is a process of exteriorization. Upright walking freed the hands, the hands freed the face, and the face made language possible. Then tools exteriorized the hand, writing exteriorized memory, and machines exteriorized operations. He even foresaw the exteriorization of the brain itself.

Each medium took something we did inside and moved it outside, into something that could do it better, cheaper, and at greater scale. Writing externalized thought. Images externalized imagination. Film externalized experiences unfolding through time.

If even biological perception is a process of modeling reality, actively constructing the world we experience, then AI media isn’t a break from human nature.

I think AI didn’t invent generation. It’s the latest thing to leave the skull, the rendering process itself. We’ve been generating throughout our entire history. The world was always rendered, and we just called it reality
